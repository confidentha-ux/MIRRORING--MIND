# Mirroring Mind — Product and UX Specification

## Product Identity

Mirroring Mind is not a psychological test, diagnosis, therapy app, or score-based assessment.

It is a quiet self-reflection journey.

The app helps users find words for their emotional experiences, notice how their emotions become interpretations, and see how those interpretations become responses.

The goal is not to fix the user.

The goal is to help the user say:

> I can see how my inner world moved today.

The app should feel like opening five inner doors and receiving a reflection card at the end.

---

## Core Product Principle

The reward is not a score.

The reward is a sentence.

The user should leave with one sentence they can carry, such as:

- I may not have been trying to attack. I may have been trying to have my feeling recognized.
- I may not only have been angry. I may have been asking to be taken seriously.
- This is not a label. It is a snapshot of how my inner world moved today.

## App Name

Mirroring Mind

Recommended subtitle:

> Find the words for what you feel.

Alternative subtitle:

> Name what you feel. See how you respond.

- choice
- pattern

- inner sentence

- another possibility

Avoid:

- test
- diagnosis
- disorder
- symptom
- clinical
- score
- ranking
- level
- therapy
- treatment
- final result

Use “choice” instead of “answer.”

Use “reflection” instead of “analysis” when possible.

> This is not a label. It is a snapshot of how your inner world moved today.

---

## Overall App Flow

1. Welcome Screen
2. Quest Map
3. Quest 1 — How You Begin
4. Quest 2 — What You Feel First
5. Quest 3 — How You Interpret
6. Quest 4 — How You Respond
7. Quest 5 — What Else Could Be True
8. Final Screen — Today’s Mirror / Reflection Card

Each quest must include:

- Quest intro screen
- Question cards
- Gentle progress indicator
- Completion reward card
- Continue button to the next quest

Do not use points, scores, rankings, levels, or competitive gamification.

Rewards should be reflection sentences, insight cards, or a Today’s Mirror card.

---

## Visual Direction

The app should feel calm, warm, quiet, and reflective.

Design direction:

- warm ivory background
- soft sage accents
- soft blue accents
- warm amber accents
- charcoal or warm gray text
- card-based UI
- generous spacing
- mobile-friendly layout
- subtle motifs of doors, mirrors, windows, cards, and soft light

Avoid:

- harsh clinical styling
- bright gamified colors
- dense form layouts
- long survey pages
- medical dashboard feeling
- competitive progress bars

The visual metaphor is:

> A quiet journey through five inner spaces.

Button:

## Quest 1 — How You Begin

Visual metaphor:

The first door.

Purpose:

Help the user notice their default way of starting.

This stage corresponds to the original “default mode / cognitive style” section.

It should identify tendencies such as:

- understanding before acting
- acting before fully understanding
- connecting through others
- protecting stability
- trusting intuition

Stage intro copy:

> The first door shows how you usually begin.
>
> Some people begin by understanding.
> Some begin by moving.
> Some look for connection.
> Some look for stability.
> Some follow the first feeling that arrives.
>
> Choose what feels closest to how you usually start.

Reward copy:

> You opened the first door.
>
> You noticed how you tend to begin.

Reward card title:

> Your Default Mode

Reward card body:

> Today, you noticed whether you tend to begin by understanding, acting, connecting, protecting stability, or trusting intuition.

## Quest 2 — What You Feel First

Visual metaphor:

The second mirror.

Purpose:

Help the user name the emotions that appear first, where they come from, and how long they stay.

This stage corresponds to the original emotional home screen section.

It should cover:

- positive emotion
- difficult emotion
- relationship emotion
- envy
- surprise
- repeating emotional patterns

Stage intro copy:

> The second mirror helps you notice what you feel first.
>
> You do not need to explain your emotions perfectly.
> You only need to choose the words that feel close.
>
> Sometimes the first step is simply finding a name for what was already there.

Reward copy:

> You turned on the second mirror.
>
> You found a few emotional words that may be close to your experience.

Reward card title:

> Today’s Emotional Clues

Reward card should summarize:

- emotion that appeared often
- emotion that stayed
- relationship feeling
- repeating emotional direction

---

## Quest 3 — How You Interpret

Visual metaphor:

The third room / inner operating system.

Purpose:

Help the user notice automatic thoughts and interpretations that appear after emotions are triggered.

This stage corresponds to the original operating system / cognitive pattern section.

It should explore:

- how the user interprets themselves
- how the user interprets others
- how the user interprets repeated situations
- how the user imagines the future

Important:

Do not present this as “cognitive distortion correction.”

Do not use clinical labels in the UI.

Instead of saying “mind reading,” say:

> How you read silence.

Instead of saying “black-and-white thinking,” say:

> How you read an outcome.

Instead of saying “overgeneralization,” say:

> How one moment begins to feel like a pattern.

Instead of saying “future prediction,” say:

> How tomorrow begins to feel in your mind.

Stage intro copy:

> The third room shows the sentences that appear after a feeling is triggered.
>
> Sometimes a feeling arrives first.
> Then a sentence follows.
>
> That sentence might be about you.
> It might be about someone else.
> It might be about what will happen next.
>
> This stage helps you notice that sentence.

Reward copy:

> You entered the third room.
>
> You noticed one inner sentence that tends to appear.

Reward card title:

> Today’s Inner Sentence

Reward card should summarize:

- sentence about self
- sentence about others
- sentence about future
- repeated interpretation

---

## Quest 4 — How You Respond

Visual metaphor:

Emotional memory / opened drawer / memory card.

Purpose:

Help the user trace one recent emotional moment.

This stage corresponds to the original memory / response pattern section.

It should trace:

- situation
- feeling
- intensity
- inner sentence
- body response
- behavior
- relationship context
- afterthought about self

This is the most important stage.

It should help users see:

> Situation → Emotion → Body → Inner Sentence → Response

Stage intro copy:

> The fourth memory opens one recent moment when your heart felt unsettled.
>
> This is not about proving who was right.
> It is about tracing what happened inside you.
>
> What was the situation?
> What feeling appeared?
> What happened in your body?
> What sentence appeared in your mind?
> What did you do next?

Reward copy:

> You opened a memory.
>
> You traced how one response moved through your body, thought, and action.

Reward card title:

> Your Response Map

Reward card should summarize:

- situation
- feeling
- body response
- inner sentence
- action
- relationship context

---

## Quest 5 — What Else Could Be True

Visual metaphor:

The fifth window.

Purpose:

Help the user look at the same memory from a slightly different angle without forcing positivity.

This stage corresponds to the original open a new window section.

It should not pressure the user to forgive, reframe, or think positively.

It simply asks:

> Could one other possibility exist?

Stage intro copy:

> The fifth window does not ask you to force a positive meaning.
>
> It simply opens a small space for another view.
>
> The same memory may still be painful.
> The same situation may still matter.
>
> But perhaps there is one more way to read it.

Reward copy:

> You opened a small window.
>
> You allowed one other possibility to appear.

Reward card title:

> One Other Possibility

Possible reflection sentences:

- Maybe I was not only angry. Maybe I wanted my feeling to be taken seriously.
- Maybe I was not trying to attack. Maybe I was asking to be recognized.
- Maybe it was not only my fault.
- Maybe the other person was also limited.
- Maybe I can hold this memory differently today.
- Maybe not knowing yet is also part of the reflection.

## Final Screen — Today’s Mirror

Purpose:

Combine the user’s choices into one non-diagnostic reflection card.

The final card is not a score, result, label, diagnosis, or personality type.

It is a snapshot.

Final screen title:

> Today’s Mirror

Final screen copy:

> Your choices have formed a reflection card.
>
> This is not a label.
> It is a snapshot of how your inner world moved today.

Final card should include:

- My default mode
- Emotion that appeared often
- Inner sentence that appeared
- Response pattern
- Core tension
- One sentence I can carry today

Final card buttons:

- Save Today’s Mirror
- Start Again
- Return to Quest Map

## Final Reflection Logic

The app should build Today’s Mirror from the user’s choices.

Local reflection is acceptable.

If using the existing `/api/analyze.js`, the prompt must follow these rules:

- Use the user’s selected choices as the only source.
- Do not infer childhood, trauma, family history, or old beliefs unless explicitly provided.
- Do not diagnose.
- Do not over-comfort.
- Do not turn the result into therapy.
- Find one strong repeated pattern only.
- Recognize a pattern only if it appears in at least two sections.
- Look for tension between strength and cost.
- If a strength in one section becomes a cost in another section, describe that tension.
- Use careful language.
- Avoid overclaiming.
- Do not say the user “is” a certain kind of person.
- Say the user’s choices “suggest,” “point toward,” or “seem to show.”

Recommended final prompt structure:

```text
You are the reflection writer for Mirroring Mind.

The user has completed five reflection quests.
Your role is not to diagnose, score, or label the user.

Your role is to find one pattern that becomes visible only when the five quests are viewed together.

Use only the selected choices provided by the user.

Rules:
- Identify a pattern only if it appears in at least two quests.
- Find one central pattern, not many.
- Notice where a strength also creates a cost.
- Do not infer childhood, trauma, family history, or hidden causes.
- Do not over-explain.
- Do not use clinical language.
- Do not use therapy language.
- Write warmly, but keep analytical distance.
- Make the reflection clear and simple.
- End with one question the user can carry.

Output structure:

What becomes visible

What this pattern may have done for you

One question to carry

Language:
English.

Tone:
calm, warm, precise, non-clinical.
```

## Question Data Direction

Use `docs/questionnaire-source-ko.md` as the source of truth for the actual question and choice content.

Translate the existing Korean questionnaire into natural English UX copy.

Do not translate literally.

The English should sound like a native app experience.

Use simple, warm, clear language.

Questions should be multiple-choice whenever possible.

Use “I’m not sure” as an option where appropriate.

Use direct input only as optional “Write my own” where necessary.

Do not shorten the questionnaire unless there is a strong UX reason.

Do not invent new psychological categories.

Preserve the five-section structure and the intent of each question and choice.
